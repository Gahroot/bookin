In the recorded current-tenant population, 12 of 147 tenants were excluded from aging results.[^E4]

The cause was in the supporting evidence. Each aging band carried the tenant’s entire open-charge history. When that exceeded the evidence limit, the whole tenant disappeared.[^E4]

I changed each band to use only its relevant charges, allowing tenants excluded by their combined histories to appear.[^E4]

I also corrected historical balance dates and property associations, and added answers from the accounting system’s own month-end ledger.[^E1][^E2][^E3]

The comparisons had defined limits: month-end balances matched for 203 of 209 tenants. Aging checks matched all current-tenant charges and former-tenant balances examined, while some former-tenant aging allocations differed.[^E1][^E5]

An individual aging band can still exceed the evidence limit and exclude its tenant.[^E4]
